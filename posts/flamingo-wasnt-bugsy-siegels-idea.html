The Flamingo Wasn't Bugsy Siegel's Idea

Aug 4, 2026 · 2 min read

Every Vegas tour tells the same story: gangster Bugsy Siegel dreamed up the Flamingo and built the modern Strip. It's a great story. It's also wrong.

The real founder

The Flamingo was the vision of Billy Wilkerson — the Hollywood publisher behind The Hollywood Reporter, who also created famous Sunset Strip nightclubs. He designed the resort and started building it on a roughly $1.2 million budget. Bugsy Siegel and his mob backers came in as the money — and eventually pushed Wilkerson out.

How it went sideways

Under Siegel, costs spiraled past $6 million — much of it owed to mob financiers Meyer Lansky and Frank Costello. The Flamingo opened the day after Christmas, 1946, and immediately bled money, losing over $500,000 in its opening weeks.

The price Bugsy paid

The property turned a profit by May 1947 — but too late for Siegel. On June 20, 1947, he was shot dead; the case remains officially unsolved. His associates took over the casino the same night. The man the legend credits with building the Flamingo didn't design it, didn't finish it, and didn't live to see it succeed.

Bugsy Siegel didn't invent the Flamingo. He inherited someone else's dream, blew the budget, and paid for it with his life.
