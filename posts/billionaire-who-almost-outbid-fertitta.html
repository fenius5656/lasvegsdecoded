The Billionaire Who Almost Outbid Fertitta

Aug 8, 2026 · 3 min read

When Tilman Fertitta agreed to buy Caesars for $17.6 billion, one name hovered over the whole deal: Carl Icahn — the activist investor who has circled Caesars for years. He got close to blowing the deal up. He never pulled the trigger.

Icahn and Caesars have history

This isn't Icahn's first move on the company. He helped engineer the 2020 Caesars–Eldorado merger that created today's Caesars, then rebuilt a stake in the years since. So when Caesars went into play again in 2026, everyone expected him to be in the room.

The bid that never landed

During the deal's "go-shop" period — the window where rival buyers can try to top the agreed price — Icahn explored countering Fertitta at around $33 a share, reportedly working through the bank Jefferies. Fertitta had already moved to about $34. In the end, Icahn never made a formal bid.

TIP
A "go-shop" is a built-in window after a deal is signed that lets other buyers make a higher offer. It's designed to prove shareholders got the best price.

How it ended

The go-shop closed in mid-July 2026 with zero rival offers on the table. Fertitta's agreement stood. Icahn blinked — and the biggest casino deal in U.S. history moved one step closer to a 2027 close.
