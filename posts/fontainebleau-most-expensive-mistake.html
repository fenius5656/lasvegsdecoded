Fontainebleau: The Most Expensive Mistake in Strip History

Aug 8, 2026 · 4 min read

One billionaire made close to $450 million off this hotel — without ever opening a door. That's where the story of the Fontainebleau Las Vegas starts, and it only gets stranger from there. This is the tower that took eighteen years to open.

An 18-year, four-owner saga

Announced in 2005, construction began in 2007. By June 2009 the tower was roughly 70% finished — then the financial crisis hit, the money ran out, and the project filed for bankruptcy.

Enter Carl Icahn. In early 2010 he bought the whole thing out of bankruptcy for about $156 million — pennies on the dollar. He never built a thing. He sold off the furniture, sat on it for seven years, and in 2017 flipped it for $600 million to a group that rebranded it "The Drew" and planned a 2020 opening. You know what happened in 2020.

Then the twist: in 2021, Jeffrey Soffer — the original developer — bought his own failed dream back for $350 million and actually finished it. It finally opened on December 13, 2023, at a total cost of around $3.7 billion — one of the most expensive resorts ever built in Las Vegas.

What it's actually like now

Here's the part the "cursed tower" headlines miss: the finished product is genuinely good. The Fontainebleau earned One Michelin Key in the 2025 Michelin Guide — the tire company's hotel rating — putting it among the most highly regarded stays in the city on its very first try.

It also quietly made history in early 2026 as the first Strip resort with its own Vegas Loop station, connecting it by tunnel to the convention center, Resorts World, Encore, and Westgate. Rides were free at launch — a real perk for a property parked at the far north end of the Strip.

The catch: that resort fee

Now the reason a stay here needs an asterisk. As of mid-2026, the Fontainebleau's resort fee runs $55/night plus tax — about $62.36 all-in. That's the second-highest resort fee in all of Las Vegas, and it jumped roughly 22% from the year before. There's also a $150/night incidentals hold to plan for.

The verdict

As a piece of Vegas history and a place to walk through, eat well, and gawk at a $3.7-billion comeback, the Fontainebleau is absolutely worth a look. As a stay, it's a beautiful room with one of the priciest fine-print bills on the Strip — go in with your eyes open on that resort fee.
