Is BLVD Worth It? The Strip's Newest Complex

Aug 6, 2026 · 3 min read

There's a brand-new, three-story complex on the Las Vegas Strip you can walk right into for free — no casino, no hotel, no resort fee. It's called BLVD, and depending on when you visit, it's either a genuinely useful free stop or a half-finished construction zone. Here's the honest read.

What BLVD actually is

BLVD is a 400,000-square-foot, open-air retail, dining, and entertainment center on the east side of the Strip — built on the old Hawaiian Marketplace site, across from the Waldorf Astoria. It's the largest standalone (non-casino) complex on the Strip. Behind it: developer Gindi Capital and the Cherng family — the founders of Panda Express, who hold about half of it.

The free stuff most visitors miss

This is where BLVD earns a stop. Almost everything good here is free:

  • 450 free self-parking spaces — nearly unheard of on a Strip that charges you to leave your car.
  • The Overlook — a ~110,000 sq ft rooftop with free public seating and open Strip views. One of the best free photo spots in town.
  • The Puma flagship's free Formula One racing simulator and digital room.
  • A giant new In-N-Out (opened June 2026) — one of the chain's largest locations.

Open stores already include Puma, Adidas, H&M, Lululemon, JD Sports, Abercrombie, and more.

TIP
Park free in the BLVD garage, ride up to The Overlook for photos, then walk the Strip from there. It's one of the cheapest "views + parking" combos on the Boulevard.

What's still coming

The headliners aren't here yet. Netflix House — a ~70,000 sq ft experience venue with a ~500-seat restaurant — plus restaurants like Tenshou are expected in 2027. Visit today for the free perks; come back in 2027 for the full thing.

The verdict

For a free stop — parking, rooftop, a snack, a walk-through — BLVD is an easy yes, especially if you're already on the south-center Strip. Just don't make a special trip expecting a finished mega-complex yet.
