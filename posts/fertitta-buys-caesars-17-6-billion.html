Fertitta Just Bought Caesars for $17.6 Billion

Aug 4, 2026 · 4 min read

It's official: on May 28, 2026, Tilman Fertitta's Fertitta Entertainment agreed to acquire Caesars Entertainment in a deal valued at about $17.6 billion — the single biggest casino acquisition in U.S. history. Here's the no-hype breakdown of the price, the structure, and what it actually means for your next Vegas trip.

The numbers

Shareholders get $31.00 a share, in cash — a 49% premium over where Caesars traded before the deal leaked. But the headline number is mostly debt:

So for every dollar going to shareholders, roughly two dollars are Caesars debt the new owner is agreeing to carry. When it closes, Caesars stops being a public company — the stock comes off the Nasdaq and goes private under Fertitta's umbrella.

Who is Tilman Fertitta?

Not a Caesars insider — an outside buyer. Fertitta already owns Landry's (600+ restaurants), the Golden Nugget casinos, the NBA's Houston Rockets, and he's the largest single shareholder of Wynn Resorts (~12%). He's also the sitting U.S. Ambassador to Italy. His owning the Golden Nugget while buying Caesars is exactly the kind of overlap regulators scrutinize.

The 8 Strip hotels changing hands

Caesars runs roughly 50 properties nationwide. On the Strip, the deal includes Caesars Palace, Paris, Planet Hollywood, Flamingo, Harrah's, Horseshoe Las Vegas, The LINQ, and The Vanderpump Hotel — plus the Caesars Rewards program.

TIP
Nothing changes for your rewards or reservations today. New-owner shakeups usually hit fees and comps first — bank Caesars Rewards value while the current rules hold.

The catch: it isn't done yet

This is a signed agreement, not a closed one. It still has to clear regulators — Nevada gaming approved two Fertitta executives in July 2026, the antitrust filing went in mid-July, and executives testified full approval could take 9–10 months, likely pushing the close into 2027. A "go-shop" window that let rival bidders top the offer closed in July with zero competing bids.
