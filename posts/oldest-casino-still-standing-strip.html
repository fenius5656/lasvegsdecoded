The Oldest Casino Still Standing on the Strip

Jul 29, 2026 · 3 min read

Las Vegas has blown up nearly every famous casino of its first generation. One survivor is left standing — and the reason it's still here is stranger than the reason all the others are gone.

The graveyard of old Vegas

One by one, the classics came down — imploded while crowds cheered:

  • The Dunes — 1993 (Bellagio's site)
  • The Sands — 1996 (the Venetian sits there now)
  • The Stardust — 2007
  • The Riviera — 2016
  • The Tropicana — October 2024, cleared for a ballpark

Through all of it, one pink neon sign never went dark: the Flamingo, which opened the day after Christmas, 1946, and is now the oldest resort still operating on the Las Vegas Strip. In 2026, it turns 80.

The twist that explains everything

Here's the trick: nothing at the Flamingo is original. Not one wall. Every piece of the 1946 building was torn down decades ago and replaced, tower by tower, while the sign stayed lit.

The casinos that got imploded froze in time until they were worth less than the land under them. The Flamingo never stood still long enough to become obsolete.

That's the whole survival strategy. It didn't outlast its rivals by being preserved — it outlasted them by being continuously rebuilt around a name and a neon sign that never left.
